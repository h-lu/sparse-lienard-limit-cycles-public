# The interval of a crest comparison

Article Lemma 4.2 supplies both (i) the single-half upper-deficit rate used across the Poincar\'e--Miranda parameter box and (ii) the matched-reference common exponent and first-order splitting for the three-match closure and the two-match transfer in Supplement Proposition 6.1. A turn radius is \(t\), and its potential coordinate is \(T=t^2/2\). The displacement is tested on compact intervals of \(0<t<g\), where \(g\) is the descending zero of the even profile. The upper intercepts of the two reference turns agree in part (ii) at later radii \(t_{b,\sigma}\to t_b>g\). Choose a finite \(T_{\max}>t_b^2/2\) after fixing the reference depth. Both actual \(T_{b,\sigma}\) lie in \(\mathcal I=[0,T_{\max}]\) for all sufficiently small perturbations. [Statement and proof](../tex/crest-estimate.tex)

## Where the hypotheses enter

1. On one half-plane let \(m_S\) and \(l_S\) be the suffix maximum and minimum of the scaled profile \(\phi\) on \([0,S]\). Integration by parts and the running-envelope inequality bound each action error by \(\sqrt{2\epsilon S}(2\|\phi'\|_{\infty,\mathcal I}+\operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal I}\phi')\). Each inner integration starts at zero, including when \(S\) is outside the test set.
2. For its own reference turn each half satisfies \(X(T_b)-X(T)=\int_T^{T_b}\epsilon e^{J_o(S)}\,dS\). Thus the upper limit of the inner action integrals ranges over all \(S\in[T,T_b]\). The envelope actions are Lipschitz in \(S\) on this finite interval. When \(T_b-T\) is bounded below, integration over a subinterval of length \(\epsilon\) adjoining a minimizer gives the logarithmic deficit error \(O(\sqrt\epsilon+\epsilon|\log\epsilon|)\). No comparison of the two reference hits enters this single-half estimate.
3. In \(u\), write \(e(u)=E(\sqrt{2u})\), \(q(u)=Q(\sqrt{2u})\). The first-order assertion is \(\|\phi_\sigma-e-\sigma\delta q\|_{C^1(\mathcal I)}=o(\delta)\). The scaled half-profiles themselves \(\phi_\sigma\) converge to \(e\) in \(C^2\) on each compact subinterval of \((0,T_{\max}]\). Near \(u=1/2\) this preserves the nondegenerate crest. Near the axis the argument uses bounded variation of the slope and the squared-slope integral on the exceptional interval. A suffix envelope there follows the profile or is constant almost everywhere. Its action is bounded by that integral. The \(C^1\) expansion gives \(\int_0^{\eta_\delta}|e'|^2\le2\int_0^{\eta_\delta}|\phi_\sigma'|^2+O(\eta_\delta\delta^2)=o(\delta)\).
4. At a shared later upper hit the two deficits at paired turns are equal. A regular ascent is identified by its strictly increasing upper height and a regular descent by its strictly increasing deficit cost. At the crest only the crest has both maximum upper height and zero deficit cost. A fixed comparison turn between \(g\) and \(t_b\) keeps paired test turns away from the reference endpoint. These facts give the common zeroth-order exponent through the crest. The first-order displacement at regular descending turns also uses the moving-level correction \(-f(a)[U(t)-U(1)]/f(t)\). This proof uses neither a uniform estimate at unbounded radius nor a second-derivative estimate for the divided perturbation. [Two matches](../tex/two-matches.tex) |

In both rows the derivative in \(u\) of \((2u)^{m/2}\) is \(m(2u)^{(m-2)/2}\). Its variation is finite on \(\mathcal I\) for each of the finitely many powers. On \([\alpha,T_{\max}]\), \(\alpha>0\), the second derivatives are bounded for every fixed exponent. Away from the axis and simple crest the limiting slope is bounded away from zero on compact branches. On the early ascent the lower powers are dominated as soon as \(Rr\ge K_0\), with \(K_0\) chosen large for the fixed coefficients. For \(x=Rr\le K_0\) the physical slope divided by \(x\) is uniformly bounded. Thus \(\phi_\sigma'=O(R^{2-d})\), and the shrinking interval of \(u\)-length \(K_0^2/(2R^2)\) contributes \(O(R^{2-2d})=o(\delta_c)\). In both rows \(\epsilon|\log\epsilon|=o(\sqrt\epsilon)\). Transverse hits follow from Article Lemma 2.2(i) for finite turns with zero linear part. Monotonicity of \(Y_\sigma\) and the envelope bounds bracket the prescribed lower-depth turns on opposite sides of their regular descending inverse.
