## Lu's classical degree construction

The version used in the article is tag `v1.0.0`, commit `17a217eb2b1832f3ea75027981d93cf57b67a360`. The public repository resolves this tag to the stated commit. Its TeX includes both papers. Both bibliographies link to the immutable source tree. The locators below use their printed numbering. The separate Zenodo DOI record could not be retrieved for this verification. [Tagged article and supplement](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/tree/v1.0.0/source)

- Article, Theorem 1.1 gives an open set in each exact degree $n ≥ 14$ with a lower bound of slope $7/2$ per degree. Proposition 4.3 gives three ordinary, three positive incoming and one negative incoming change with one even and one odd power. [Degree theorem](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/blob/17a217eb2b1832f3ea75027981d93cf57b67a360/source/main.tex) · [One-sided well](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/blob/17a217eb2b1832f3ea75027981d93cf57b67a360/source/tex/wells.tex)
- For the deduction per primitive term summarized in Article §1.3 and detailed in Supplement §1.1, use the profile in Article, Lemma 5.3 before continuing its sequence of remote odd powers. At $s$ separated radii the three lists give at least $7s,3s,s$ changes. Lemma 3.2(ii) preserves them upon adding the next-to-leading odd power. Propositions 3.1 and 2.4(i) realize and regularize the ordinary returns through the linear coefficient. Proposition 3.3 closes the incoming list. This gives $11s+1$ cycles with at most $3s+9$ powers. Writing $m=8+4s$, Proposition 3.3 permits the exterior powers $m+1,m+2,m+3$ after the highest inner odd power $m-1$ is chosen. The degree is at most $4s+11$. [Separated radii](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/blob/17a217eb2b1832f3ea75027981d93cf57b67a360/source/tex/remote-powers.tex) · [Realization and closure](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/blob/17a217eb2b1832f3ea75027981d93cf57b67a360/source/tex/transfer.tex)
- Article (3.2)–(3.3) distinguish the two actions within a fold window. Article (3.11), (3.17), Proposition 3.3 and Lemma 3.4 already compare gaps to the same reference on each half. An entry estimate and control of the distortion from a common terminal section give physical accuracy $o(1)$ under their hypotheses. Supplement (S5.9) imposes three matches. Equations (S5.12)–(S5.13), on p. 16, give the first-order correction on the descent. Supplement Proposition S1.2 supplies the exact-degree examples used in the two comparison tables. [Exterior transfer](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/blob/17a217eb2b1832f3ea75027981d93cf57b67a360/source/tex/transfer.tex) · [Terminal-section estimate](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/blob/17a217eb2b1832f3ea75027981d93cf57b67a360/source/tex/terminal-distortion.tex) · [Degree-twelve matches](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/blob/17a217eb2b1832f3ea75027981d93cf57b67a360/source/tex/sextic-and-joint-cap.tex) · [Low-degree proposition](https://github.com/h-lu/classical-lienard-degree-public/blob/17a217eb2b1832f3ea75027981d93cf57b67a360/source/low-degree-supplement.tex)

For the truncated comparison, $s$ is any integer at least one. Taking $s=\lfloor(k-9)/3\rfloor$ gives the finite floor bound for $k\ge12$ by persistence. The two full one-radius rows of Proposition 5.1 use different supports. At $h=1,e=0$, twelve allowed powers in degree 14 give $11+10+2=23$. At $h=1,e=1$, thirteen allowed powers in degree 15 give $12+10+2=24$. In both cases powers 4 and 6 are absent. In particular, degree 67088 gives 234784 when all powers are allowed. The lower bounds of 19 at degree 12 and 22 at degree 13 are exact-degree results of Supplement Theorem S1.1 and Proposition S1.2. The article's Corollary 1.2 alone gives 18 at degree 12. ## Palomar's isolated cycles

The unsigned preprint's Theorems 1–2 give the sextic coefficient box and the exact-degree lower bound. Its explicit septic is obtained by adding $10^{-41}x^7$. The trapping argument supplies a common return domain between successive strict samples. Appendix A supplies the six rational sextic coefficients, seven heights and fourteen half-return intervals. The SHA-256 of the cited TeX file is `b0510754d992edb87d6f7751335b85e0c45922a7f6725fdbbc039a1ff13e6cd1`. Passing that file to `verification/check_full_support.py --source-tex` compares the digest and all 28 interval endpoints. The original certified integrations are the preprint's. Supplement §1.1 derives hyperbolicity at nearby coefficients by varying the linear term and gives the subsequent odd-power extensions. [Pinned original](https://github.com/mmislan/palomar-lienard-limit-cycles/blob/7da225327c6cd62a3dc63926525377764ea8f454/paper/main.tex)

## Local return series at a fixed center

Françoise's journal paper identifies the generators of the Bautin ideal in Theorem 1.1 and the triangular leading coefficients in Lemmas 1.3–1.4, pp. 257–259. The arXiv preprint uses different numbering. Article Remark 2.4 applies the published identities at zero trace, treats a linear primitive term by the multiplier, and derives its fixed-support bound by analytic division and Wronskians. This application counts shrinking cycles at one even center. [Published paper](https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/mrl/2002/0009/0003/MRL-2002-0009-0003-a003.pdf) · [Preprint version](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0110132)

## Rotation, first variation and trinomial results

De Maesschalck and Dumortier identify rotation by each odd coefficient on pp. 2163–2164. For a rising classical profile, Proposition 1 of De Maesschalck and Huzak gives the first variation with their orientation. Their Theorem 1 is the exact-degree comparison, and §3.1 preserves earlier simple zeros when adding powers. Both papers are journal publications. [De Maesschalck and Dumortier](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jde.2010.12.003) · [De Maesschalck and Huzak](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10884-014-9358-1)

Chen, Jin, Li and Zhou's Theorem 1.2 and its proof give three simple return matches on the support {1,9,10}. The regular horizontal level in §3 gives three hyperbolic cycles. For the same support, our Supplement §3 proves the degree-ten case of its trinomials of all even degrees $d\ge10$. In particular, the finite hyperbolic nest required by Article Proposition A.1 is available within the submitted pair. The balanced seed for the valley construction has different exponents. [Three-cycle construction, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19874v1)

Chen, Lu, Wang and Zhang's Theorem 4 treats $a_1x^{2N}+a_2 x^{2m+1}+x^{2n+1}$, for integers $2\le N\le m<n$ and $a_1a_2\ne0$. It states absence for $a_2>0$ and exactly one hyperbolic attracting cycle for $a_2<0$. Their §3 credits Cen and coauthors with absence for the positive sign and existence for the negative sign before proving uniqueness and hyperbolicity. [Theorem 4 and proof, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19873v1)
